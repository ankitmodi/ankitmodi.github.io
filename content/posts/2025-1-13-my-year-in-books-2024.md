Here's what I read in 2024 - in order of what I liked most (highest rated first):

### <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/28695456-drug-hunters' target='_blank'>1. Drug Hunters: The Improbable Quest to Discover New Medicines</a>
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1457528227i/28695456.jpg" alt="cover image" height='300' width='200px' style='float:left; padding-right:20px; padding-bottom:5px; padding-top:5px'>
Author: <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/1629971.Donald_R_Kirsch' target='_blank'>Donald R. Kirsch, Ogi Ogas</a>
<br>
My rating: ★★★★★
<br><br>
"Drug Hunters" is an enthralling dive into the world of drug discovery, spanning from ancient healers to today's big pharma. The stories of scientific breakthroughs (as unrelated as dye production) dramatically transforming drug development are gripping. I particularly loved the story on contraceptive discovery, blending scientific ingenuity & societal challenges.
It’s a vivid, engaging read that appeals to both experts & casual readers, offering a unique lens on the art of scientific zeitgeist and its life-saving impact.
<br clear="all"><br>



### <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/193401835-how-prime-ministers-decide' target='_blank'>2. How Prime Ministers Decide</a>
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1691245783i/193401835.jpg" alt="cover image" height='300' width='200px' style='float:left; padding-right:20px; padding-bottom:5px; padding-top:5px'>
Author: <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/17127788.Neerja_Chowdhury' target='_blank'>Neerja Chowdhury</a>
<br>
My rating: ★★★★★
<br><br>
This book gives a fascinating insight into the complex decision-making in the Indian politburo. Every decision is an intricate balancing act and the immense personal will required to push through reforms is crazy. The book is impressively well-researched and manages to maintain an unbiased perspective. The impact of efficient decision-making or the lack of it is truly mind-boggling.
<br clear="all"><br>



### <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18050143-zero-to-one' target='_blank'>3. Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future</a>
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1630663027i/18050143.jpg" alt="cover image" height='300' width='200px' style='float:left; padding-right:20px; padding-bottom:5px; padding-top:5px'>

### <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/49680197-parasitic-mind' target='_blank'>8. Parasitic Mind: How Infectious Ideas Are Killing Common Sense</a>
<img src="https://images-na.ssl-images-amazon.com/images/S/compressed.photo.goodreads.com/books/1585294777i/49680197.jpg" alt="cover image" height='300' width='200px' style='float:left; padding-right:20px; padding-bottom:5px; padding-top:5px'>
Author: <a href='https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/403686.Gad_Saad' target='_blank'>Gad Saad</a>
<br>
My rating: ★★★★☆
<br><br>
“The Parasitic Mind” by Gad Saad is refreshingly candid and unapologetically direct, devoid of any political correctness. Saad offers a compelling glimpse into the frustrations felt by the political right toward the extreme left, passionately arguing that the “pursuit of truth” should always take precedence over “not hurting feelings”. His examples highlight how scientific rigor has sometimes declined in the face of this fear. The witty and humorous writing style makes this a fun read - especially in light of incidents at the recent Olympic games.
<br clear="all"><br>
